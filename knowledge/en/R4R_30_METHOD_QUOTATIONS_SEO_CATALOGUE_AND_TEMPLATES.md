# Riansares 4R — method, quotations, SEO, catalogue and templates

## 15. Sound estimating

### 15.1. The price per m² comes at the end

Correct order:

1. define scope;
2. measure each job;
3. separate substrates and methods;
4. calculate materials;
5. estimate labour;
6. include logistics;
7. include waste;
8. include access equipment and other site resources;
9. include coordination;
10. add contingency and margin;
11. divide by m² only for analysis.

Do not start with a rate per m² and force the work to fit it.

### 15.2. Commonly forgotten items

- protection;
- moving furniture;
- loading and transporting rubble;
- travel;
- accommodation;
- purchasing and delivery;
- drying;
- finishing details;
- junctions;
- seals;
- cleaning;
- coordination;
- warranties and snagging;
- tools and consumables;
- hidden damage;
- material compatibility.

### 15.3. Margin and profit

Gross margin is not net profit. It must cover:

- self-employment contributions or staffing overheads;
- vehicle;
- accounting and administrative services;
- insurance;
- telephone;
- customer acquisition;
- quotation preparation time;
- warranty;
- incidents;
- unbilled days.

Do not count a margin included in a worker’s rate and another business margin twice as though they were independent profits.

### 15.4. Reduce scope rather than give work away

When the client does not accept the amount:

- remove less urgent interventions;
- retain usable elements;
- separate optional items;
- propose phases;
- explicitly change quality levels;
- do not retain the same scope with a discount that eliminates margin and quality.

### 15.5. Preliminary and final quotations

#### Preliminary

- range;
- subject to a visit;
- few items;
- explanation of risks;
- confirm whether the order of magnitude fits.

#### Final

- measurements;
- materials;
- included scope;
- exclusions;
- payment terms;
- timeframe;
- warranty;
- hidden damage;
- optional items;
- revision and date.

## 16. Damp: general framework

### 16.1. Diagnosis before product

Do not start with “which paint to use”. Start with:

- where it appears;
- height;
- orientation;
- relationship with the ground;
- pipes;
- roof;
- rain;
- ventilation;
- temperature;
- salts;
- previous treatment;
- active or historic damp.

### 16.2. Compatible materials

For old damp or salt-affected walls, appropriate options may include:

- lime mortars;
- rehabilitation mortars;
- renovation mortars for damp and salt-affected substrates;
- mineral finishes;
- breathable paints.

They must be applied to a sound substrate after incompatible or loose material is removed.

### 16.3. Solutions that must not be sold as miracles

- anti-damp paint over a leak;
- gypsum plaster over a salt-affected wall;
- plasterboard bonded to a damp wall;
- mesh as a universal structural solution;
- textured stipple coating to hide major unevenness;
- breathable mortar as elimination of the source;
- internal waterproofing without analysing external water.

### 16.4. Useful content for clients

Explain:

- the difference between repairing a finish and eliminating the cause;
- why a wall may take time to dry;
- why stains reappear;
- what a sound substrate means;
- why trial openings are made;
- what the warranty covers;
- when a technical professional is needed.

## 17. Diagnosis and selective demolition

Principle:

> Demolish only when there is a technical reason or the scope requires it.

Examples:

- do not strip out a bathroom if the leak is already repaired;
- do not remove sound tiles behind a wall lining if they do not interfere;
- do not demolish an old house’s layout if it can be improved safely;
- do not replace an extractor before testing it;
- do not dismantle an entire cistern if the aim is a temporary repair;
- do not remove a sound existing concrete floor base without analysing levels and adhesion.

Advantages:

- lower cost;
- less waste;
- less risk;
- less time;
- less collateral damage;
- a more defensible quotation.

## 18. Client communication

### 18.1. Short quotation message

Structure:

1. greeting;
2. provisional diagnosis in one sentence;
3. included work;
4. price or range;
5. main condition;
6. closing.

### 18.2. Correcting an error

Structure:

1. acknowledge the specific error;
2. identify the affected item;
3. provide the new total;
4. retain the technical recommendation where appropriate;
5. avoid excessive justification.

### 18.3. Client pressing on price

Response:

- explain cost drivers;
- propose reduced scope;
- separate optional items;
- do not disparage other quotations;
- do not accept responsibility without sufficient payment;
- clearly withdraw if infeasible.

### 18.4. Riansares 4R WhatsApp

It should sound natural:

- short sentences;
- few technical terms;
- visible price;
- simple condition;
- “I’ll let you know beforehand” for extras;
- “let’s see whether it’s easy to resolve” when not yet inspected;
- do not send a three-minute report by chat.

## 20. Measurements and sketches for reconstructing plans

Geometrical interpretation errors appeared several times in the communal entrance, office toilets and other sketches. The editorial lesson is that a convincing image is not equivalent to a correct plan.

### Data to collect

1. Name of each room and route to reach it.
2. Consistent orientation: entrance, rear, left and right, without changing reference points.
3. Total length of each wall and breakdown into sections.
4. Thickness of walls, arches, columns and partitions where relevant.
5. Doors: width, height, distance from the corner and opening direction.
6. Windows: width, height, sill height and distance from corners.
7. General clear height and height changes.
8. Steps, ramps and level changes, separate from the room plan.
9. Visible services: stacks, electrical panels, radiators, sanitary fittings, drains and ducts.
10. General photographs, photos of each wall and of junctions.

### Useful description format for an LLM

- Start with the general shape: rectangle, L, irregular room or group of rooms.
- Describe the route from the entrance.
- Describe walls clockwise.
- Identify the object of each measurement: “wall”, “door opening”, “arch thickness” or “height”.
- Mark each datum as `confirmed`, `approximate` or `not measured`.
- Separate plan, elevation and section.
- Do not automatically turn a stair measurement into a wall measurement.
- Do not invent diagonals or add sections not defined as consecutive.

### Checks before accepting the plan

- Section totals must match total length within measurement tolerance.
- Doors and windows must fit within the wall.
- The drawing must respect which elements are inside or outside the room.
- Horizontal and vertical dimensions must not be interchanged.
- Drawing proportions must correspond to measurements.
- Residual gaps must be explained.
- Opening directions must not obstruct routes or sanitary fittings.
- Uncertainties must remain visible rather than being hidden by a clean drawing.

- Which photos a technical professional needs before a visit.
- How to describe an irregular room to an LLM.
- Common mistakes when measuring doors, arches and stairs.
- Plan, elevation and section: basic differences.

## 34. Minimum technical glossary

- **Concrete floor base:** base layer prepared to receive flooring.
- **Floor finish:** final flooring laid over the substrate.
- **Welded wire mesh:** metal reinforcement mesh; must be installed on spacers according to design.
- **Contraction joint:** cut or joint intended to control shrinkage cracking.
- **Perimeter joint:** separation at junctions with vertical elements.
- **Breathable mortar:** mortar compatible with vapour release; it does not eliminate active water.
- **Renovation mortar:** system for damp or salt-affected substrates, subject to specification and preparation.
- **Independent wall lining:** internal lining independent of the existing wall.
- **Moisture-resistant board:** board resistant to humid environments, not waterproof by itself.
- **Phenolic panel:** compact panel resistant to moisture and impacts, used in cubicles and intensive-use areas.
- **Stain-blocking primer:** product blocking stain migration before finishing.
- **Substrate consolidant:** consolidates a powdery substrate; does not correct deeper adhesion failure.
- **Float or fill valve:** mechanism filling the cistern and shutting off water.
- **Flush mechanism:** mechanism releasing water into the toilet bowl.
- **Angle valve:** local wall-mounted shut-off valve.
- **Flexible connector:** flexible connection between supply and fixture.
- **Hidden damage:** defect not visible before demolition, opening up or stripping back.
- **Work item:** specific economic and technical unit in a quotation.
- **Site resources:** protection, tools, scaffolding, consumables and resources needed for execution.
- **Contingency:** reserve for reasonable uncertainties, not duplicated profit.
- **NHL:** natural hydraulic lime; sets hydraulically and is often compatible with old masonry when correctly specified.
- **Triple-twist mesh:** flexible galvanised mesh adaptable to vaults and irregular surfaces.
- **Centring:** temporary formwork supporting an arch during construction and setting.
- **Inspection access:** removable access for inspecting, cleaning or repairing concealed services.
- **Compression coupling:** removable mechanical joint; must remain accessible and unstressed when used as a maintenance point.
- **PVC solvent welding:** solvent joint between compatible components; not a filler for gaps.
- **MS polymer:** high-performance elastic adhesive-sealant used in certain compatible installations.
- **Unpriced bill of quantities:** measurements without prices, or a preliminary estimate not automatically equivalent to an execution offer.
- **Lift building works:** pit, shaft and structure; do not necessarily include car, machinery, installation or regulatory approval.
- **Construction compromise:** solution accepted because of actual constraints that must not become a universal rule.
- **Beyond capacity:** work whose risk, structure, equipment or responsibility exceeds available resources.

## Extension — real questions, quotations and evidence (November 2026)

### Address the client’s intent first

For “what should I do about a leak?”, begin by stopping the leak and checking safety; for “can I replace tiles?”, begin with conditional feasibility; for “how much does it cost?”, request measurements, scope and quality levels. Never automatically answer with the price of a 7 m² kitchen when nobody said the kitchen measures 7 m².

### Distinguish technical references from commercial prices

International guides (EPA, NARI, NAHB, HSE) provide general good practice, not local prices, commercial warranties or binding rules in Spain. Price depends on measurements, design, permits, materials and date. Extended source: `sources/R4R_71_INTERNATIONAL_TECHNICAL_REFERENCES_2026.md`.

### Bilingual evaluation before publishing new examples

Repeat equivalent Spanish and English questions about the same kitchen, compare retrieved excerpts and citations, and record when the answer abstains. Fluency does not demonstrate documentary support; abstention may be correct when evidence is absent. Store actual run results and models, rather than assumptions.
