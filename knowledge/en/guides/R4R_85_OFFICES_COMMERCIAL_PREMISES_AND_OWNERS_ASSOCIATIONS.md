# Riansares 4R — offices, commercial premises and owners’ associations: scope and coordination

## What changes when renovating an occupied office?

Phases, working hours, noise, dust, access, signage, safety, continued use and work areas must be agreed. Before quoting, define floor area, number of workstations, services, fire protection and other requirements for the activity and premises. Do not assume the requirements are the same as for a home.

## Can corporate toilets be renovated without closing the entire office?

It may be feasible if the area can be isolated and services, access and alternative facilities ensured. Assess water interruptions, waste, ventilation, noise and out-of-hours work. Execution depends on the building and safety requirements, as well as floor area.

## What should be checked when converting an office into a meeting room?

Occupancy and use, possible accessibility and escape requirements, lighting, ventilation, heating and cooling, acoustics, electricity and data cabling. Apparently lightweight partitions may conceal services or have fire protection implications.

## What documentation should be requested for commercial premises renovations?

Address and municipality, current and proposed activity, plans, photos, measurements, hours, expected occupancy, existing services, owner or owners’ association authorisation where appropriate, and licensing requirements. A renovation proposal does not certify administrative compliance.

## What should be done about a leak in a communal entrance hall?

Confirm its source (roof, downpipe, façade, communal or private pipe), inform the owners’ association and document damage. Resolve the cause before repairing finishes. Specific liability depends on the facts and legal framework; it is not determined by the stain’s location.
