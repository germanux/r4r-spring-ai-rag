# Kitchens — itemised quotations and change control

## What should a kitchen renovation quotation include?

Describe scope, units and measurements, dismantling, rubble, building work, services, furniture and finishes as appropriate. State materials, exclusions, payment terms and an indicative timeframe subject to availability and findings.

## Why distinguish repair from renovation?

Repair addresses a functional or safety need; renovation may incorporate optional improvements. Separating them enables the owner to identify urgent items and those that can wait.

## What changes when hidden damage is discovered?

Document the finding, explain why it changes scope, detail alternatives and obtain agreement before carrying out additional non-urgent work. Avoid unspecified, uncommunicated charges.

## How can tiling be priced without knowing the square metres?

Measure actual surfaces and openings; define format, justified wastage, finishing details, removal of tiles and substrate. Without these dimensions, only non-binding guidance can be offered.

## Can appliances be chosen at the end?

They should be specified before openings, connections and ventilation are installed, even if purchased later. Changing equipment dimensions at the end may require modifying services or cabinets.

## What conditions distinguish included materials from client-supplied materials?

Clarify who purchases, transports, stores, bears breakage costs, checks compatibility and handles warranties. Labour should not automatically include responsibility for the performance of materials not supplied.

## How should a provisional quotation be handled?

Use known items and measurements, identify items subject to inspection and explain what information is missing. Avoid suggesting a fixed quotation when concealed services have not yet been opened up.

## Why set aside a contingency?

Renovations of existing buildings can reveal hidden problems. An agreed contingency supports planning but does not authorise extras without justification of their need and acceptance.

## What should a change order contain?

Description of the finding and modification, previous/new scope, price or measurement method, schedule impact, responsible parties and acceptance. Keeping photos and appendices prevents later confusion.

## How can quotations from two companies be compared?

Check that they include the same work, quality levels, units, permits where appropriate, schedules, warranties and waste management. A lower total with broad exclusions may not be truly comparable.

## Case P-01 — Tile change during execution

**Hypothetical assessment case, not completed work or a service committed to by Riansares 4R.**

**Situation:** The client replaces the previously chosen finish.

**Checks:** Availability, compatibility, wastage and work already completed.

**Possible scope:** Change order showing material price difference and date.

**Decision criterion:** Do not assume the modification is cost-free.

**Evidence to retain:** Acceptance and delivery notes.

## Case P-02 — Discovery of a damp wall

**Hypothetical assessment case, not completed work or a service committed to by Riansares 4R.**

**Situation:** Removing a cabinet reveals previously undetected damp.

**Checks:** Measure the extent and diagnose the source.

**Possible scope:** Separate proposal for repair and finishing.

**Decision criterion:** Do not conceal damage with a new finish.

**Evidence to retain:** Photographs, diagnosis and agreement.
