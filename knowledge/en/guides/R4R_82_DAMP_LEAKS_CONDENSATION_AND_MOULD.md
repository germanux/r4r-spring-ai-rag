# Riansares 4R — damp: differential diagnosis and repair

## How can condensation, water ingress, rising damp and plumbing leaks be distinguished?

Condensation is usually associated with surface temperature, indoor water vapour and ventilation; water ingress may correlate with rain, the roof, façade or ground; rising damp may affect the lower parts of porous walls; a plumbing leak may follow pipework or service routes. Signs overlap. Photographs and stains do not establish a definitive diagnosis: observe timing, height and seasonality and carry out the relevant checks.

## What should be done when water appears on a wall after rain?

Inspect and document roof junctions, gutters, downpipes, the façade, terrace, windows and doors, and joints, as well as water paths. Avoid concealing the symptom with paint. Resolve the entry point before reinstating finishes. A watertightness test or technical inspection may be necessary.

## Can a ceiling stained by a leak be painted?

First check that the leak has been repaired and the substrate is dry and sound. Depending on the damage, prepare stained areas, apply a stain-blocking primer and paint the entire surface for a uniform appearance. Do not paint over active dripping. Replace degraded boards or finishes where appropriate.

## Does lime mortar eliminate damp?

No. It may be compatible with historic substrates and support appropriate hygrothermal behaviour, but **it does not stop active water ingress**. Selection requires examining wall composition, salts, adhesion, compatibility and the source of damp. Consult the applicable requirements of [CTE DB HS](https://www.codigotecnico.org/pdf/Documentos/HS/DBHS.pdf).

## Why does mould return even after cleaning?

Because the moisture source may remain. EPA recommends repairing leaks and promptly drying wet surfaces and materials; cleaning alone does not remove the cause. As general preventive guidance, addressing wet materials within 24–48 hours helps reduce the risk of mould growth, without guaranteeing the absence of problems. [EPA guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## What should be done about longstanding damp in a house with thick walls?

Review the ground and external levels, downpipes, water ingress, salts, ventilation and previous waterproof treatments. Use compatible materials and carry out local tests before stripping back everything. Do not recommend injections, membranes or barriers as universal solutions without evidence.

## Can a damp wall be concealed with a wall lining?

It should not be covered without identifying and controlling the source. This may hide damage and hinder ventilation or maintenance. If a wall lining system is appropriate, its design must accommodate moisture risks, insulation and substrate needs.

## Hypothetical case H-01 — Damp near a window after window replacement

**Hypothetical example.** After windows are replaced, droplets appear on cold walls. Assess actual ventilation, thermal bridges, heating use and possible leakage around the windows. The solution might require ventilation and thermal improvements; do not automatically diagnose rising damp.

## Hypothetical case H-02 — Courtyard façade with detached render

**Hypothetical example.** After rain, flaking appears near a downpipe. Check drainage and cracks, stop the water supply, allow appropriate drying and only then reinstate render and paint.
