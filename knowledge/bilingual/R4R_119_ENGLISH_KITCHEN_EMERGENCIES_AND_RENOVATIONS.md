# English renovation Q&A — kitchen plumbing, leaks and renovation choices

## My kitchen pipes are broken. What should I do?

If water is leaking actively, close an accessible and safe water shutoff valve, keep away from wet electrical equipment, and contact a qualified plumber. Locate and repair the leak, check for hidden moisture, then restore finishes after the installation is tested. This is general guidance, not a diagnosis of your property.

## Should I renovate the entire kitchen because a pipe broke?

Not necessarily. A localized repair may be enough if the rest of the pipework is serviceable. A broader renovation is an optional decision; compare pipe condition, access, desired layout and budget.

## I also want to renovate the kitchen. What do you recommend?

Repair the damaged plumbing first, assess water and drainage connections, then decide whether to change layout, wiring, tiles, cabinets or appliances. Coordinate hidden installations before finishing walls and floors. Keep safety repairs separate from optional upgrades.

## Can I replace the wall tiles too?

Yes, provided any leak is repaired and the substrate is sound and appropriately dry. Old loose tiles or damaged surfaces may need removal and preparation. Tiling is a separate renovation item; it does not stop leaking pipes.

## Can I keep my existing cabinets?

Possibly, if they are undamaged and can be removed or protected while the pipes are repaired. Check access, dimensions and whether water damage has weakened panels before reuse.

## Can I replace cabinets and appliances while repairing pipes?

Yes, but it is optional. Confirm water, drains, electric circuits, dimensions and ventilation for the selected appliances. Pipe repair alone does not require new cabinets or appliances.

## Should I move the kitchen sink?

Only after checking that supply and drainage routes, gradients, access and connections can be adapted. Keeping the sink can simplify work, but a measured layout review is needed.

## What if a socket is wet after a kitchen leak?

Do not touch wet electrical equipment or attempt repairs on energized circuits. Keep people away and request qualified electrical assessment before reuse.

## Will the insurance pay for the damage?

That depends on policy terms, coverage and cause. Document the incident and ask the insurer. Do not assume every leaking pipe is covered.

## Can you give me an exact kitchen-renovation price?

Not without measurements and scope. Floor/wall area, existing installations, materials, labor, waste and access affect the quotation. A sample price for another kitchen must not be presented as your price.

## What should come before new kitchen tiles?

Identify and repair leaks, complete concealed plumbing and electrical work, verify the installations, and prepare suitable dry substrates. New finishes should be installed only after these checks.

## Can the kitchen stay in use during work?

That depends on the extent of demolition, temporary water/electricity cuts, dust and safety. Plan alternatives for food preparation during periods when the kitchen is unavailable.
