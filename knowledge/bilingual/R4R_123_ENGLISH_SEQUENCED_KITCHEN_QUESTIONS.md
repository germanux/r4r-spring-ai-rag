# English questions for a progressive three-step kitchen inquiry

## Step 1 — I have broken pipes in my kitchen. What should I do?

First address active water leaks safely: isolate water if accessible, keep away from wet electrical installations and contact qualified help. Inspect and repair pipework, check hidden water damage, and postpone permanent finishes until the repair is verified. This is not a remote diagnosis.

## Step 2 — I have broken pipes in my kitchen. What should I do? I also want to renovate my kitchen. What do you recommend?

First repair and verify the plumbing. Then map the desired kitchen layout and decide whether to change wall tiles, electrical points, cabinets or appliances. Separate necessary repairs from elective renovation; keep access to water and drains for maintenance.

## Step 3 — I have broken pipes in my kitchen. What should I do? I also want to renovate my kitchen. What do you recommend? Can I replace the tiles too?

Yes. Replace the tiles as part of the renovation if the pipes have been repaired and tested and the wall or floor substrate is in suitable condition. Tile replacement is optional and should be quoted separately from plumbing; it does not repair hidden leaks.

## Clarification — Does replacing kitchen tiles require changing every pipe?

No, not automatically. Evaluate the condition and accessibility of the existing pipework. If only a localized repair is needed, other serviceable runs can remain unless a broader change is justified.

## Clarification — Can I upgrade the cabinets as well?

Yes, if the dimensions, installations and budget are coordinated. Repairing a broken pipe does not make new cabinets mandatory.

## Clarification — What if I cannot close the water shutoff?

Avoid unsafe manipulation of pressurized installations and request professional assistance to isolate the supply. Keep people away from wet electrical equipment.

## Clarification — Are the English and Spanish questions the same evidence?

They can be semantically equivalent even if phrased differently. For retrieval, preserve meaning, constraints and technical terms; do not treat translated text as proof or add new property details.

## Clarification — Does the assistant remember the prior question?

These example questions intentionally repeat and append previous details in the same text. They are independent searches, not proof of conversational memory.

## Clarification — Is the recorded answer guaranteed to match a new live answer?

No. Model output and retrieval order may vary; the goal is factual consistency and sources relevant to the current question.

## Clarification — Should the visitor replace the entire kitchen?

Only if the desired scope and assessment justify it. Water safety and pipe repair take priority; replacing finishes, furniture and appliances is optional.

## Clarification — What should be shown as supporting evidence?

A cited document should support the specific claim, such as checking and repairing leaks before finishes, not an unrelated case study or a sample kitchen budget.

## Clarification — Can you quote an exact price for my kitchen?

Not without sufficient measurements, site context, materials and scope. An illustrative 7 m² budget must not be quoted as a real price for an unspecified kitchen.
